Research

Practical Attacks on a Decentralized Secure Messenger Session

USENIX WOOT ’26Kota Urushigaki, Hayato Kimura, Atsushi Tanaka, Takanori Isobe

Session is a decentralized, anonymous end-to-end encrypted messenger with more than a million downloads on Google Play alone. In late 2020 it moved away from the Signal Protocol to its own design, which a 2021 Quarkslab audit covered only partly because the switch came near the audit’s end. Working from the shipped implementation, I analyzed its 1-to-1 and closed-group messaging and found two design flaws: public keys are never mutually authenticated, and messages are not cryptographically bound to monotonic counters.

From these I built three new practical attacks, usable by a malicious storage node or an unprivileged insider:

  • impersonation by substituting a contact’s public key on first contact,
  • forging message timestamps to reorder a conversation,
  • silently dropping or replaying messages.

The paper also proposes concrete mitigations. Its artifact received the USENIX Artifact Available badge.

I disclosed all three attacks to the Session team. In late April 2026 they accepted most of our findings, and classified the replay attack as unexploitable because an incidental database configuration currently blocks it, rather than a designed security boundary. As of July 2026, fixes were in progress.

Docker · Python · Swift (iOS) · Repository

A fully local, reproducible testbed for all three attacks. A Docker Compose stack runs five honest storage nodes and one malicious node whose attack hook can be switched at runtime; three patched iOS clients play sender, receiver and attacker; and a single command-line tool brings up the network and drives each attack.
